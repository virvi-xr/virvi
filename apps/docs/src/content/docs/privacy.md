description: What Virvi analytics collect in Decentraland scenes, and how to delete your data.

## What is collected
When a scene uses Virvi, and you accept its notice (below), the scene sends pseudonymous usage events to Virvi while you're inside its parcels: when you arrive and leave, where you walk (sampled every few seconds), what you click or hover, videos you play, emotes, and camera mode.

## How you're identified
- By default Virvi stores a **per-scene pseudonym**: a keyed hash of your wallet (or guest id). It can't be linked across scenes and it isn't your wallet address.
- **Display names and wallet addresses are only stored** if the scene owner turns on wallet visibility. When they do, the scene shows a notice when you enter, and only data collected after that notice includes your wallet and name.
- **IP addresses are never stored.** Virvi uses them briefly to limit abuse and to look up your country, then discards them.

## Your choices
- **In scenes:** the first time you enter a Virvi scene, a small notice asks whether Virvi may record how you use it. Until you choose, nothing is sent. If you decline, nothing is recorded for the rest of your visit, including which ads you saw and your wallet and name in a scene that has wallet visibility on. Ads still show; they just aren't counted for you. Raffles, BEATs and giveaways still work, under their own agreements. One choice covers every Virvi scene, and you're asked again only when the notice changes. To change your mind, press Privacy at the bottom right of the screen. Scenes that haven't been updated since the notice was added don't show it; Virvi still records nothing from them for a visitor who has declined.
- **Your choice is recorded:** each time you choose, Virvi keeps what you chose, which version of the notice it was, the scene and platform, and when. It's kept under a keyed hash of your wallet (or guest id), separate from the per-scene pseudonym and never your wallet address, so one choice can cover every scene. It's kept for as long as Virvi runs scenes; a deletion request (below) removes it, except that a final "decline" is kept so it keeps being honoured.
- **In the dashboard:** when you're signed in, Virvi asks once whether it may record errors in your browser and the pages you visited just before them (see Error reporting). If you decline, your browser records and sends none. Until you choose, nothing is sent. Change it at any time under Privacy in Settings. Each choice is kept with its version and time, while your account exists, and is deleted with it. Errors our server records are unaffected: they cover no browsing.

## Retention
Raw events are kept for the scene owner's plan (7–365 days, unlimited on the top tier and self-hosted installs), 30 days for unclaimed scenes, and 7 days for local previews. Aggregated statistics (counts, heatmaps) are kept longer and contain no identities.

## Terms of Service agreements
When you agree to the Terms of Service, Virvi records which version you agreed to, when, and whether you agreed when signing up or later. The record is kept while your account exists and is deleted with it.

## Virvi Summit sign-ups
When you sign up for the Virvi Launch Summit at virvi.io/summit, Virvi stores your email address, your name, how you’ll join (online, in person or with a watch party), your party size or your watch party’s city and expected guests, the sessions you’re interested in, where you came from (the ref in the link you followed, if it had one), and when you agreed to Summit emails. It’s used only to send you emails about the Summit and to plan the event, and only Virvi staff see it. If you sign up again with the same email, the new answers replace the old ones. Your IP address is used briefly to limit repeated sign-ups and is not stored. It’s all deleted by 31 August 2027. To have it deleted sooner, email support@virvi.io.

## Deleting your data
Call `POST /api/analytics/me/delete` with your session token. A dashboard control for this is coming. This removes your sessions, events and positions from every scene, your ad records, your raffle entries, your BEAT data (see BEATs below for tokens already sent), and the record of your choices on the scene notice (a final decline is kept, so it keeps being honoured).

## Raffles
A scene owner can run a raffle. Nothing about you is entered unless you choose to enter and agree to the raffle terms, and only while signed in with a verified wallet inside that scene.
- **What's shared, and with whom:** your display name (or a shortened wallet address if you have no name) is shown on the raffle wheel to everyone in the scene. Your name and wallet address are shared with the scene's host so they can deliver your prize. Other visitors never see full wallet addresses (a visitor with no name is shown as a shortened wallet like 0x1234…abcd).
- **Check-ins:** while you are entered, the scene sends a check-in every 30 seconds so the host can tell who is present. Check-ins stop when you withdraw or the raffle ends, and only your total time present and when you were last seen are kept.
- **Withdrawing:** you can withdraw while the raffle is open or drawn, in the scene or in Settings under Raffle entries (which also lists your entries and wins), and withdrawing deletes your entry. Past draw records keep only an anonymous entry id, so they still check out; a withdrawn winner shows there as "Removed entrant" and is left out of later draws.
- **Retention:** 30 days after a raffle ends, its entries (with your name and wallet address) are deleted. Its draw records keep only anonymous entry ids.
- **Deleting your data:** deleting your data (below) also deletes your raffle entries.

## BEATs
A BEAT is a free token that shows you were at an event. Nothing about you is stored unless you choose to track your attendance and agree to this, while signed in with a verified wallet inside the event's scene (to claim with an account in the Virvi app instead, see BEATs in the Virvi app below):

> To track your attendance for the {name} BEAT, Virvi stores your wallet address, your display name and how long you're here. The scene checks in every 30 seconds while you're in it. The hosts of this event will see your name, wallet and time. If you claim, your wallet, this BEAT and the time it was sent are recorded on Polygon, a public blockchain: anyone can see that record, and nobody can delete it. You can stop tracking at any time before you claim, which deletes what was stored.

Most hosts leave **Claim automatically** on. Then the text also says "Agreeing also claims this BEAT to your wallet once you've been here long enough.", and agreeing also claims the BEAT for you as soon as you've been there long enough, so your wallet is recorded on Polygon without a second step.

- **What's stored:** your wallet address, your display name, and how long you were present at the event (from a check-in every 30 seconds while you're in the scene; time only counts while the scene is connected to Virvi's live room). To check that your wallet has a Decentraland profile, Virvi asks Decentraland's public profile server about your wallet address when you track and when you claim; only whether a profile exists is kept, for up to a day. To allow only a few claims per network (currently 3), Virvi keeps a count per BEAT under a scrambled code (a keyed hash) of your network, never your internet address and not linked to your claim; the counts are deleted once the BEAT's claims are over.
- **Who sees it:** the event's hosts (the BEAT's creator, and anyone who can run events in its scenes) see your display name, wallet and whether you claimed, even when the scene's wallet visibility for analytics is off, because you agreed to it for the BEAT. Virvi staff can also see them, to fix problems.
- **On the blockchain:** once your BEAT is sent, the token, your wallet address and when it was sent are recorded on Polygon, a public blockchain. Anyone can see them, and they can't be deleted, by you or by Virvi. The BEAT's name, picture and place are served by Virvi.
- **Stopping:** you can stop tracking at any time before you claim, which deletes what was stored. If the host cancels the BEAT, what tracking stored is deleted too.
- **Retention:** if you opted in but never claimed, your data is deleted 30 days after the BEAT ends. The list of wallets in each transaction Virvi sends is deleted 30 days after it settles (or 30 days after it was made, if it never settles); the transaction's id stays. If you claimed, your wallet address and display name are kept with your BEAT until you delete your data (above). When a BEAT or giveaway item reaches your wallet, your account gets a notice in the dashboard's bell naming it; these are kept 90 days, can be turned off in Settings, and go when you delete your data. If a host removed your claim and you then delete your data, Virvi keeps only that claim's wallet address, so it can't claim that BEAT again, until 30 days after the BEAT ends; hosts don't see it.
- **Deleting your data:** deleting your data (above), or your account, deletes your BEAT data that hasn't been sent, though your wallet address can stay in Virvi's transaction records for up to 30 days. For a BEAT already sent, the token stays in your wallet and on the blockchain, but Virvi removes your display name, how long you were there and the link to your account. A BEAT being sent at that moment is hidden from the host at once; once the send settles, it is deleted if the send failed, or unlinked like this if it arrived.
- **A lost or stolen wallet:** you can ask Virvi to move your BEATs to a new wallet (Settings, Move my BEATs to a new wallet). Virvi stores both wallets, your reason, your account, and how your session signed in when you asked or cancelled; reviewers see these with your account's name and email, and you see the reviewer's note. Virvi also records when and how each wallet was linked to your account, and unlinks and blocks. If approved, the old wallet is blocked from your account and from BEATs (it can't sign in to your account, its sessions there are refused, and it can never collect a BEAT again), your API keys and paired devices are removed, and a reviewer may also remove and block other wallets linked while signed in with the old wallet. The move is recorded on Polygon for good: anyone can see that the old wallet was replaced by the new one for that BEAT, and the Safe's signers see both wallets in the transaction file. The old copy stays in the old wallet and can never move. Hosts see your new wallet marked as reissued. Requests are deleted 30 days after they close, or when you delete your data; if you delete your data while a move is under way, Virvi keeps only the two wallets, the BEAT numbers and the state, with no link to you, until it finishes or is withdrawn (and for 7 days after a withdrawal, in case the transaction goes through anyway). A blocked wallet stays blocked after you delete your data or your account: Virvi keeps only its address and why it was blocked.

## BEATs in the Virvi app
In the Virvi app (iPhone) you can earn a BEAT in an AR space, by time present while the space is shown, or in person, by scanning the event's printed ARC near the venue. Nothing about you is stored unless you choose to track your attendance and agree to this:

> To track your attendance for the {name} BEAT, Virvi stores your account, how long you're here and, when you claim, the wallet you choose. Virvi checks your location only when you check in and when you claim, and doesn't keep it. The hosts of this event will see your name, your time and, if you claim, the wallet you choose. If you claim, your wallet, this BEAT and the time it was sent are recorded on Polygon, a public blockchain: anyone can see that record, and nobody can delete it. You can stop tracking at any time before you claim, which deletes what was stored.

- **Signing in:** you sign in to the Virvi app with your Virvi account, through virvi.io's sign-in, which includes Sign in with Apple. Apple may give Virvi a private relay email instead of your own. Virvi treats that email as verified and never merges the account with another one by email.
- **What's stored:** your account, how long you were present (in an AR space, while the space is shown; at an in-person event, from your check-in) and, when you claim, the wallet you choose. Minutes from Decentraland and from AR spaces add up toward one claim. You get one claim per BEAT. If you have several wallets, you choose one for each BEAT.
- **Location:** the app reads your location only at two moments: when you check in to an in-person BEAT and when you claim it. It is compared with the event's venue pin and is not stored.
- **App Attest:** Apple's device check proves a request comes from the genuine Virvi app. Virvi stores a key made for that install of the app, not your Apple ID or a device identifier. While you have an account, the key is linked to it. If you delete your BEAT data or your account, the key itself stays, and once your account is deleted it has no account linked. A BEAT that was already sent keeps the key's id next to its public wallet, so one install can't claim the same BEAT twice.
- **Waiting claims:** if you have no wallet linked, your BEAT is saved and holds one of the limited copies. It is sent when you link a wallet, and sent again if the first try failed, when you link the wallet again or open your collection. It is kept until it's sent, the host removes it, or you delete your data or your account, and it is never lost to the 30-day clean-up.
- **Venue maps (for hosts):** when you set a venue pin for an in-person BEAT, your browser loads the map's tiles from OpenStreetMap, which sees your IP address and the area you are looking at. When you choose Suggest from address, Virvi sends the venue address to OpenStreetMap's Nominatim service to suggest a pin. Visitors' locations are never sent to OpenStreetMap.
- **Quest headsets:** you sign a headset in by entering its code at virvi.io/pair or in the Virvi app on your phone. On a Quest you can earn BEATs in AR spaces; in-person BEATs are phone-only. The headset's sign-in tokens are stored encrypted on the headset.
- **Stopping and deleting:** you can stop tracking before you claim, which deletes what was stored. Who sees your data and what goes on the blockchain are as described under BEATs above. Deleting your data or account deletes your BEAT data that hasn't been sent, as described there, but not the install key described above.

## Virvi Spaces on Quest
Virvi Spaces is the Meta Quest app that shows a Virvi space in your room. It talks to virvi.io. Pictures, videos and 3D models in a space load from wherever its owner hosts them, which may be outside virvi.io; those hosts see the headset's network address like any website would.
- **Every visitor sends:** the code of the ARC you look at, to find its space; a request for the space's contents (the space feed); and anonymous visit events: when you arrive and leave, and where your head is (every few seconds), in the space's own coordinates rather than your room's. Each visit uses a fresh random id that is never stored, so visits can't be linked to you or to each other.
- **Builders also send:** the edits they make to a space, the alignment of an ARC, and the pairing of their headset (including the headset model) with a Virvi account. The headset proves who it is with a secret kept in its encrypted storage; the server stores only a hash of it.
- **Never sent:** camera images and room scans. The headset's cameras are used on the headset to find ARCs, and your room's walls and surfaces are used on the headset to hide virtual things behind real ones and to snap items to walls. Neither leaves the headset.

Visit events follow the same retention as the rest of Virvi analytics, above. They carry no identity, so there is nothing to look up or delete for a single visitor. A builder can revoke a paired headset on virvi.io, which stops it from editing.

## Error reporting
When something goes wrong on virvi.io, the page (if you allowed it; see Your choices) and our server record the error so we can find and fix it. A report holds the error message, the page, the build of Virvi, your browser and operating system, your organization and scene ids, the time, and the last few things you did: pages you opened, the names of buttons and links you clicked, and the requests the page made (their address, result and timing). Before anything leaves your browser, tokens, email addresses, wallet addresses, secrets and the query part of web addresses are removed. It never includes what you type, passwords, wallet signatures, payment details, or what the page sent or received, other than the error message that came back.

- **How you're counted:** by a keyed hash of your account id, so we can see how many people an error reached without storing who. To count how many different places an error comes from, we also keep a hash of your network address made with a random key that changes every day. The key and those hashes are deleted after 2 days, after which they can't be traced back to an address or linked to another day. Your address itself is never stored.
- **How long:** error details, the account hashes and the breakdowns by page, browser and release are kept for 30 days. Hourly counts of each kind of error, with no identity, are kept for 400 days.
- **If you send a report:** when a page breaks it records the error without your account. If something you were doing fails and we need more detail, we may ask you about it, at most once a day; you can turn that off in Settings (or from the message itself). Whether you report from there, a crash page or Report a bug in the menu, you see exactly what will be sent before you press Submit, and nothing is sent before that. A report you send (what you wrote, that preview and a screenshot if you add one) is linked to your account if you're signed in, so we can follow up. Reports are kept for 400 days, and screenshots for 30 days. Ask us at support@virvi.io to have one deleted sooner.
- **Asking only when it helps:** to ask you at most once a day, and to say "we know about this" only once for each problem, we keep when we last asked you and which problems we told you about by the account hash above (never your account itself), for 30 days, and on your account only whether you turned asking off; if you're not signed in, this browser keeps it.
- **Our issue tracker:** when automatic bug filing is turned on, the details of errors that keep happening (the redacted messages, pages, steps and counts above) are sent to Linear, the issue tracker we use, as bug reports, without your account. A report you choose to send is added to that bug with what you wrote (with email addresses and similar details removed) and, if you're signed in, your account id, never your name or email. Screenshots stay with us and are not sent to Linear. Those Linear comments aren't removed by the 400-day limit or when you delete your account; ask us at support@virvi.io and we'll remove them.

## Assistant

The Virvi Assistant on the dashboard keeps your conversations with it until you delete them. To write its replies, what you type and the dashboard information it looks up for you (such as your scenes and their settings) are processed by Anthropic. When the assistant changes something for you, we keep a record of the change so you can undo it. That record stays after you delete the conversation, without the message text. See the [assistant guide](/docs/dashboard/assistant/) for what it can do.

**AI agents with an API key:** when an agent you connect uses Virvi through its MCP server, Virvi records each tool it used, the scene it named, whether it worked and when, against that key. You see this under Settings, API Keys, Activity. It's kept 90 days and goes when you delete the key.

## Advertiser early access
When you ask for advertising early access on virvi.io, Virvi stores the email address, name, company or project, website and message you enter, and the time you sent the form. It's used only to contact you about advertising on Virvi. If you send the form again with the same email, the new answers replace the old ones (fields you leave blank keep what you sent before). Your IP address is used briefly to limit repeated submissions and is not stored. To be removed from the list, email sales@virvi.io.
